Current work

Failure Modes and Fair Evaluation of Precision Curricula for In-Hand Reorientation

self-directed study Isaac Lab · Shadow Hand, Allegro, BrainCo Revo3 · PPO

Code

Competence-gated curricula are everywhere in dexterous manipulation and almost never examined. Pushed toward a precision limit they fail in a specific, structural way: a noisy competence estimate clears the promotion threshold by luck, difficulty steps beyond what the policy can sustain, the success signal starves, and a monotone gate has no way back. The run sits in an absorbing state and never recovers.

Adding a single demotion branch removes the failure entirely: zero collapses in ten seeds where a fair, equally-denoised monotone gate still loses five. Scoring every policy at a common fixed tolerance shows what that does not buy. No scheduler tested masters 0.01 rad. Bidirectionality delivers a reliably finished run, not a higher precision ceiling. A variance-aware gate designed to suppress premature promotions showed no detectable advantage across five replications: when a curriculum can undo its mistakes, caution about making them is redundant.

Thesis & automation work

Before the robot-learning work, I built production machines. My master's thesis became a label attachment machine that ran on a factory floor: a Delta SCARA robot placing 6 × 6 mm labels onto PCBs, with the picking point found by Canny edge detection, Hough straight lines, and their intersections, then mapped into robot coordinates through a perspective transform. Mean positioning error was 0.11 mm in x, 0.07 mm in y, and 0.90° in rotation, at roughly 180 ms for seven labels.

It also taught me the lesson the rest of my work follows from. With the dedicated light source, every label position was detected 100% of the time. Remove it and leave the ambient factory fluorescents, and detection fell to 83% at the near positions and 25% at the far one. The algorithm did not change. Only the light did. That is the brittleness of hand-specified perception, and it is why I moved to learning-based methods.
